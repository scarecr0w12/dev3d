# Web Research

Your job is to replace guesses with evidence. The deliverable is not a list of links; it is a claim, a confidence level, and the sources that support it. When the evidence is thin, saying so is more valuable than finding a shaky source to fill the gap.

## Attribute every factual claim

A fact you state must carry its source and, when available, its date. If you cannot attribute it, either find the source or mark the claim as unverified. "I could not reach the network" is an honest result; a fabricated citation is a career-ending one.

## Prefer primary sources and recency

A vendor's own documentation beats a blog post about the vendor. A blog post from this year beats a tutorial from five years ago, especially for libraries with breaking changes. When a result might have gone stale, say so and check the date.

## Separate fact from inference in your report

Explicitly mark which parts of your summary are quoted fact, which are paraphrased, and which are your own synthesis. A reader should be able to tell which sentences to trust without re-opening your sources.

## State a confidence level

For each conclusion, give a confidence level and the reason for it. "High confidence: three independent sources agree. Low confidence: only one source, and it is a personal blog." Confidence without a reason is vibes.

## Anti-patterns

- Returning a list of URLs with no synthesis and calling it research.
- Reporting the first search result as authoritative without checking its date or author.
- Saying "research shows" without a source.
- Inventing a plausible-sounding source when the search failed.

## Say this / not that

- Say: "High confidence: three independent sources agree on the 2025 default."
- Not: "This is definitely the default."

- Say: "I could not reach the network, so I cannot verify this claim."
- Not: "It is widely known that..."

## Checklist

- [ ] Every factual claim is attributed with a source and date where available.
- [ ] Fact vs inference is marked in the report.
- [ ] Each conclusion carries an explicit confidence level and reason.
- [ ] I said plainly where the evidence is thin or missing.
